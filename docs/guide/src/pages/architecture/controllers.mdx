# Controlled notes

A controlled note requires **two independent authorizations** to move: its owner proves ownership, and its controller approves the proposed transition. The note commitment binds its controller and policy, so the owner cannot remove that condition while spending it.

Controlled notes use the same reusable receiving addresses and encrypted delivery as ordinary push payments. A recipient can be offline. When the wallet discovers the payment, it also discovers its controller and policy. It must show those restrictions before treating the value as available to spend.

Ordinary notes have zero controller and policy fields and need no controller approval.

## Two independent authorities

The owner's local proof establishes ownership, membership, nullification and conservation. The controller's signature approves a specific use of that value. Neither authorization substitutes for the other.

For example, Alice holds 1,000 controlled units. With approval she sends 600 to offline Bob and keeps 400 as change. Bob restores his wallet from seed, then requests approval to send 500 to Dave, keep 60, and withdraw 40 publicly. Bob's ownership proof and the new controller approval are both required.

All inputs and outputs in an operation retain the same controller. The controller can approve output policies, but the reference policy preserves its configured policy commitment. Controlled and ordinary inputs are not mixed in one spend. A withdrawal releases tokens to the approved public destination; the note condition does not follow those tokens outside the pool.

The controller cannot spend a note using its signing key. It can, however, withhold approval or become unavailable and prevent the owner from moving that value. Seed recovery restores ownership; it does not remove policy restrictions.

## What the controller learns

The controller receives a scoped request containing input nullifiers, amounts and policies; output commitments, amounts, policies and encrypted-delivery digests; and withdrawal destinations and amounts. It also sees chain, pool, root, token, controller, expiry and nonce.

It does not need the owner's spending key, receiving identities, note randomness, or Merkle paths. This is still a deliberate disclosure of transaction details to that controller. Public observers also see which controller a controlled spend uses. The controller does not provide ordinary-note anonymity from its own observations.

## Exact approval

The private statement recomputes a transition digest from the actual proven legs. The controller signs that digest using an agreed encoding. Authorization binds:

- Protocol, chain, pool, root, token and controller.
- Block-number expiry and a request nonce.
- Ordered input nullifiers, amounts and policies.
- Ordered output commitments, amounts, policies and ciphertext digests.
- Actual withdrawal destinations and amounts.

The pool checks the signature and expiry before settling. Nullifier consumption prevents replay. The nonce distinguishes requests; the reference has no separate revocation registry.

## Two commitments, different jobs

The **statement binding** connects the proof to the exact public operation. Changing a ciphertext or withdrawal destination cannot reuse that proof.

The **controller transition digest** connects policy approval to the actual private amounts and policies as well as the public destinations and delivery. An owner who obtains a valid new proof for a changed withdrawal destination still needs a new controller approval. Both commitments must agree with the executed operation.

Delivery remains atomic, and recipients still verify decryption, note commitments and canonical history. A valid approval is not evidence that the intended recipient successfully received a decryptable payment.

## Reference and production boundary

`bench/controlled-push-e2e/` implements a distinct controlled-push protocol in a fresh pool. `SignatureController` verifies real Stark-curve ECDSA against an immutable public key. Its off-chain fixture authorizer checks context, policy, conservation, expiry and an approved withdrawal destination and limit. The exact encoding and evidence are recorded on the [status page](/status#controlled-payment-validation).

This is a narrow policy fixture. Application-specific policies, key rotation, revocation, controller availability, and any escape mechanism need explicit design. There is no unilateral escape in this reference. Independent cryptographic review and integration into the maintained packages remain required.
