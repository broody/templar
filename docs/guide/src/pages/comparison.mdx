# Compared with Starknet Privacy

Templar is based on **StarkWare's Starknet Privacy pool**. Its defining change is to remove the auditor and screening roles and keep private spend inputs on the holder's device. That choice also changes the note model, client responsibilities, and application surface.

This comparison describes upstream source at [commit `b40bf10`](https://github.com/starkware-libs/starknet-privacy/tree/b40bf109c8e5b97d9d6e8ec6db48b16b9d10a106), checked against `main` on **20 September 2026**. It is a comparison of implementations, not a claim about every deployed pool's configuration. Templar's implementation is experimental; the [status page](/status) records what has been demonstrated.

## What they share

Both are token pools on Starknet with private note ownership, nullifiers to prevent reuse, off-chain computation, and public settlement. Both use Cairo, Poseidon-based derivations, and native proof verification with proof facts connecting a proven virtual execution to contract actions. Upstream describes this division in its [architecture overview](https://github.com/starkware-libs/starknet-privacy/blob/b40bf109c8e5b97d9d6e8ec6db48b16b9d10a106/README.md#architecture).

Templar proves on the user's device: either the whole spend as a native PROOF1, or a compact client proof that a server wraps into PROOF2 without seeing the witness ([proving architectures](/architecture/proving)). The key privacy distinction here is **who receives the private inputs before proving**. A zero-knowledge proof can hide a witness from public observers while the service generating that proof still sees it.

## Trust and data access

| Boundary | Starknet Privacy | Templar |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Auditor | Viewing-key registration encrypts the user's private viewing key to a configured auditor key. | No global auditor key or mandatory auditor disclosure path. |
| Deposit screening | Regular pool deposits require a screener attestation; open-note funding uses configured screening policies. | No screener key or screening-attestation check. |
| Proving | The documented SDK flow sends private invocation inputs to a proving service. | The holder proves locally. An optional wrapping server receives only an already zero-knowledge client proof. |
| Discovery | The supplied discovery service accepts per-request decryption keys and returns decrypted results. | Recipients download encrypted public outputs and decrypt locally; full viewers track spent status. Scalable private synchronization remains unqualified. |
| Administrative authority | Role-based governors, pausing, and contract replaceability are present. | The prototype owner can change verifiers, fees or anonymizer allowlists. Configuration trust remains. |

The auditor path is explicit in [viewing-key registration](https://github.com/starkware-libs/starknet-privacy/blob/b40bf109c8e5b97d9d6e8ec6db48b16b9d10a106/packages/privacy/src/privacy.cairo#L319-L356). The SDK includes `user.viewingKey` in [proof invocation calldata](https://github.com/starkware-libs/starknet-privacy/blob/b40bf109c8e5b97d9d6e8ec6db48b16b9d10a106/sdk/src/internal/proof-invocation-factory.ts#L136-L149). Discovery documents its [per-request keys and decryption boundary](https://github.com/starkware-libs/starknet-privacy/blob/b40bf109c8e5b97d9d6e8ec6db48b16b9d10a106/crates/discovery-service/README.md#key-design-decisions), while the contract documentation describes [administrative controls](https://github.com/starkware-libs/starknet-privacy/blob/b40bf109c8e5b97d9d6e8ec6db48b16b9d10a106/packages/privacy/README.md#security).

### Screening has a specific scope

Upstream derives a screening subject from the applied actions. A regular deposit selects its funding address; an operation with no screening subject does not require an attestation. Open-note funding has `Required`, `Exempt`, and `Delegated` policies. It would be inaccurate to describe every upstream private transfer as needing a screener's approval. See the [action checks](https://github.com/starkware-libs/starknet-privacy/blob/b40bf109c8e5b97d9d6e8ec6db48b16b9d10a106/packages/privacy/src/privacy.cairo#L858-L943) and [screening service documentation](https://github.com/starkware-libs/starknet-privacy/blob/b40bf109c8e5b97d9d6e8ec6db48b16b9d10a106/proof-interceptor/README.md).

Templar removes that protocol role. Optional [policy notes](/architecture/policy-notes) put application rules on particular notes through an on-chain controller and a deadline refund; ordinary notes have none.

Templar's adapter pins the virtual OS and statement identity and matches the proven public message to the intended pool and spend. The pool separately checks accepted roots, unused nullifiers, actions, fees, and transfers. See [proofs and verification](/architecture/proofs).

Transport privacy is a different layer. Upstream's [OHTTP support](https://github.com/starkware-libs/starknet-privacy/blob/b40bf109c8e5b97d9d6e8ec6db48b16b9d10a106/crates/discovery-service/README.md#ohttp-oblivious-http) can separate client IP information from request contents; the destination service still receives the contents it must process. Templar likewise needs to address RPC query patterns and network metadata even with local proving.

## Notes and supported flows

| Area | Starknet Privacy | Templar |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Receiving a payment | Registered viewing keys, sender/recipient channels, and token subchannels | Recipient shares a locally derived cloaked receiving address, with no pool registration; payer creates and publishes an independently encrypted note |
| Note storage | Notes are stored in a map and read during virtual execution | Commitments are leaves in a depth-32 tree; private membership paths prove inclusion |
| Spending ownership | The owner's private key participates in nullifier derivation | The receiving identity binds spending-derived authority and nullifier material; spending still requires the private spending secret |
| Deposits | Deposit actions in the proven flow and internal funding of open notes from adapter responses | Standalone proofless deposit that transfers funds, computes the commitment, and publishes the encrypted note |
| External applications | External invoke/anonymizer flows, open notes and `ComputeAndInvoke` (`privacy_compute`) | Proof-bound external calls with open notes, including upstream's shadow-account anonymizer; upstream-compatible adapter responses become ordinary funded tree notes; no general `privacy_compute` |
| Spend shape | Client action phases cover registration, channels, deposits, notes, withdrawals, and invokes | One public token per spend: up to 4 inputs, 4 outputs and 2 withdrawals (PROOF1), or 2 inputs, 2 outputs and 1 withdrawal (split, R0); policy notes settle through their own relation |

Upstream's [contract interfaces and action phases](https://github.com/starkware-libs/starknet-privacy/blob/b40bf109c8e5b97d9d6e8ec6db48b16b9d10a106/packages/privacy/README.md#client-action-phases) describe these flows. Its [note lookup](https://github.com/starkware-libs/starknet-privacy/blob/b40bf109c8e5b97d9d6e8ec6db48b16b9d10a106/packages/privacy/src/privacy.cairo#L607-L630) and [nullifier formula](https://github.com/starkware-libs/starknet-privacy/blob/b40bf109c8e5b97d9d6e8ec6db48b16b9d10a106/packages/privacy/src/hashes.cairo#L224-L235) show that recipient ownership is already enforced there. Both designs enforce recipient ownership: creating a note for someone does not give its payer the right to spend it. Templar separates that ownership rule from independent encrypted delivery to a reusable address.

The tree and delivery design solve different problems. Templar's commitment tree supports proving membership without naming the consumed note; receiving keys and encrypted publication enable asynchronous delivery. Map storage does not inherently require channels, and a Merkle tree alone does not provide push payments. Templar's tree itself uses storage maps.

For Templar's side of the comparison, see [notes and ownership](/architecture/notes), [pool state](/architecture/state), and the [transaction lifecycle](/architecture/lifecycle).

## What the trade-offs mean for developers

**More work moves to the client.** Local proving removes the need to entrust a proving service with the witness, but the user's device must supply the compute and memory. Full client-side proving runs in 5.84–6.31 GiB and about 23 seconds with bounded memory; the split architecture's client proof takes about 2–5 seconds and 0.3 GiB, with the heavy PROOF2 wrapping (about 2 minutes, 28 GiB) moved to a server that never sees private data. Physical device and browser qualification of complete payments remain open. There is no matched upstream benchmark, so these figures do not establish a speed or cost advantage. [Measurement scope](/status#device-memory).

**Receiving uses public encrypted history.** Templar publishes encrypted openings with funded commitments. Recipients can receive offline and recover from seed plus history. The wallet must authenticate and synchronize that history efficiently; the current small-pool scanner is not a scalable private discovery service. Upstream supplies channel-based discovery infrastructure, and its SDK can open channels automatically as part of preparing a payment. Channels therefore do not inherently require a separate user-visible handshake.

**Application scope is explicit.** Templar's [open notes](/architecture/open-notes) support atomic external funding through the upstream adapter interface, and persistent positions through upstream's shadow-account anonymizer. The destination is transaction-local; the funded result is an ordinary tree note. Upstream fills stored open notes during its action flow. Upstream's general `privacy_compute`, which runs an application's Cairo over the user's secret, has no equivalent on the compact client proof. [Policy notes](/architecture/policy-notes) are a separate capability for rule-governed assets.

**Security evidence has its own scope.** Upstream publishes an [audit record for a specific contract revision](https://github.com/starkware-libs/starknet-privacy/blob/b40bf109c8e5b97d9d6e8ec6db48b16b9d10a106/docs/audit/README.md). That review does not automatically cover Templar's statement, commitment model, or adapter. Templar's current evidence is Sepolia push-payment, recovery and open-note lifecycles with full client-side proving, local end-to-end runs of the split architecture with real proofs, and internal reviews and tests; governance, client, token, and production qualification are still required. [Current status](/status).

Both systems also expose public settlement metadata. Removing trusted privacy actors does not hide deposit and withdrawal timing, eliminate transaction-sender correlation, or remove reliance on Starknet and token contracts. Those boundaries are described in [privacy and trust](/architecture/privacy).
