# Private value. Public verification.

<p className="guide-lead">Templar is a private token pool on Starknet with push payments to reusable cloaked receiving addresses. Share a cloaked address, receive while offline, use public DeFi without revealing which notes you spend, and prove spends on your own device.</p>

Templar is based on [StarkWare's starknet-privacy](https://github.com/starkware-libs/starknet-privacy), with its auditor role removed, deposits screened against sanctions lists before admission, and private validity proved on the user's device. No proving operator ever receives users' secrets. The [trust model](/architecture/privacy#trusted-roles) explains these boundaries and the remaining authority of pool configuration and deposit admission.

The [Starknet Privacy comparison](/comparison) shows how the two designs differ in trust, note handling, supported flows, and client responsibilities.

The pool holds ERC20, ERC721 and ERC1155 tokens. A user's private balance is the sum of the unspent **notes** they control. Each note describes some value and a spending condition. The chain stores a cryptographic commitment to the note rather than a private balance indexed by wallet address.

To pay someone, a holder consumes existing notes and creates fresh encrypted notes for the recipient and, when needed, change. A zero-knowledge proof establishes ownership, membership in the pool, and conservation of value. The pool binds the proof to the exact operation and publishes the encrypted outputs atomically. The recipient discovers them later using their wallet keys.

## Four ideas to keep in mind

| Idea | What it means in Templar |
| --- | --- |
| A note is a claim on pooled value | Spending requires its opening and the owner's spending secret. |
| A commitment records creation | A hash binds the note's value and spending conditions into the pool's tree. |
| A nullifier records consumption | A derived public identifier can be used only once, without directly naming the consumed tree leaf. |
| A proof authorizes specific changes | The proof is bound to the pool, chain, public statement, and exact action list. |

These concepts are similar to consuming and creating outputs in a UTXO system. The additional work is proving which hidden inputs are valid without disclosing their openings or their positions in the tree.

## Three kinds of note

| Note | What it is | Spent by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Ordinary note](/architecture/notes) | A private claim on an amount of one asset (ERC20, ERC1155, or an ERC721 NFT), owned by a receiving key | The owner's spending secret |
| [Open note](/architecture/open-notes) | A destination for the proceeds of an external call (a swap, a vault), funded in the same transaction | Becomes an ordinary note once funded |
| [Policy note](/architecture/policy-notes) | Funds that may only move into outcomes committed at creation, approved by an on-chain controller, refundable after a deadline | Anyone holding its capability, only as the policy allows |

## How spends are proved

The device proves only the payment rules, in a small, browser-class circuit, and the proof is zero-knowledge before it leaves the device. A wrapping server that never sees private data turns it into a native Starknet large proof. [Proving](/architecture/proving) explains the split and a future device-side path.

## Scope

This guide explains the architecture and its security boundaries. Everything described is experimental and unaudited.

Ordinary spends, open-note swaps and policy-note escrow run end to end locally with real client proofs and real large proofs. On Sepolia, deposits are on chain and a proof-carrying swap succeeds in simulation; submission awaits the gateway's acceptance of large proofs. Production integration, independent review and device qualification remain open. The [implementation status](/status) page identifies what is demonstrated and what remains unqualified.
