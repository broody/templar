# The circle group

## The FFT needs a group of size 2ⁿ

Fast polynomial arithmetic (NTT/FFT — turning coefficients into values and
back in `n log n` time) needs an evaluation group with a power-of-two size
and a nested sub-group structure: size 2³¹ split cleanly into 2³⁰, 2²⁹, …
down to 2. Classical STARKs get this from **roots of unity**: in a field
where `p − 1` is divisible by 2³¹, the element `ω = g^((p−1)/2³¹)` generates
such a group.

## Why M31 cannot do that

The multiplicative group of a field has order `p − 1`. For `p = 2³¹ − 1`:

```
p − 1 = 2³¹ − 2 = 2 · (2³⁰ − 1)
```

The power-of-two part of `p − 1` is just **2¹**. The largest "FFT-shaped"
multiplicative subgroup has two elements. Usable for nothing.

## The fix: use a different group

Over M31, the solutions to

```
x² + y² = 1
```

form a group of exactly **2³¹ points** under a tangent-style addition law —
the **circle group**. Concretely: `p ≡ 3 (mod 4)` means `−1` has no square
root, and over such fields the unit circle has `p + 1 = 2³¹` points. A
generator `G` generates the whole group, and it has the nested structure
FFT needs: points of order `2ⁿ` sit inside points of order `2ⁿ⁺¹`.

So this stack runs the entire polynomial machinery — trace domains, FRI
domains, the "vanishing polynomial" — on **circle cosets** instead of
root-of-unity subgroups. The names change (`CanonicCoset`, `odds(n)`,
coset trees), the roles do not: a domain of size `2ⁿ` is a coset of the
order-`2ⁿ` subgroup.

Details you will meet in Part 3, translated:

| Classical (roots of unity) | This stack (circle) |
| --- | --- |
| subgroups of `F_p*` of size 2ⁿ | order-2ⁿ subgroups of the circle |
| evaluation domain = subgroup | domain = a coset of the subgroup |
| FFT twiddles `ω^k` | "circle twiddles" from point doubling |
| vanishing polynomial `xⁿ − 1` | a degree-2ⁿ circle vanishing polynomial |

Two structural facts worth remembering, because they matter for privacy in
Part 5:

1. A trace of `2ⁿ` rows lives on the coset `odds(n)`; its low-degree
   extension lives on the larger coset `odds(n + blowup)` — and **these two
   cosets share no points**. The verifier samples the big coset, so raw rows
   are never opened directly.
2. Every opened value is a weighted sum of the row values with publicly
   computable weights. "Openings leak linear combinations" is a property of
   this geometry, and it is the hinge of the privacy analysis.

> **Check.** In Python, count solutions of `x² + y² ≡ 1 (mod 7)` for
> `x, y` in `0…6`. You should find 8 — and indeed `p + 1 = 8` when
> `p = 7 ≡ 3 (mod 4)`. Then try `p = 11 (≡ 3 mod 4)` and confirm 12 points.

## What to carry forward

1. M31's multiplicative group is FFT-unusable; the circle group fixes it.
2. All domains are circle cosets of power-of-two size.
3. Trace domain and evaluation domain are *disjoint* cosets — geometry that
   becomes privacy-relevant later.
