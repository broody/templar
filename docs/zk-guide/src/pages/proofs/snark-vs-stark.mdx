# SNARK vs STARK

## SNARKs: tiny proofs, borrowed trust

SNARKs (Groth16, PLONK, Marlin…) commit to polynomials with **pairing-based
cryptographic objects** (KZG-style polynomial commitments over elliptic-curve
groups).

- **Proof size**: hundreds of bytes. **Verification**: milliseconds, constant.
- **Zero-knowledge: native.** Blinding factors are mixed into the polynomial
  commitments as part of the scheme — hiding is built into the math, not an
  add-on. This is why "ZK-SNARK" is almost redundant: a well-built SNARK
  hides by default.
- **The cost: a trusted setup.** The commitments rest on secret parameters
  ("toxic waste") that must be generated once and destroyed; Groth16 needs
  this *per circuit*. Multi-party ceremonies mitigate but complicate.
- **Post-quantum: no.** Pairings break under quantum algorithms.
- **Field inflexibility**: the circuit's arithmetic lives over an
  elliptic-curve field (BN254, BLS12-381). Computations in *other* fields —
  say felt252, Starknet's 252-bit prime — must be **emulated** limb by limb,
  multiplying cost by tens.

## STARKs: big proofs, no trust

STARKs commit with **hashes** (Merkle trees, Part 2.2) and prove low-degree
structure with **FRI** (Part 3.2).

- **Transparent**: no setup, no ceremony — the system's security is
  hash-function security.
- **Post-quantum-friendly**: hash-based.
- **Proof size**: tens to hundreds of kilobytes (measured in this repo:
  ~232 KB for a payment proof, vs ~0.2–8 KB for SNARK-class proofs).
- **Zero-knowledge: not native.** Nothing in the hash/FRI machinery hides
  the witness; hiding must be added deliberately. This asymmetry — soundness
  free, hiding expensive — is Part 5's subject.
- **Arithmetic freedom**: the base field is a design choice made for *speed*
  (M31! Part 1.1), and foreign-field computation can instead be structured
  around it.
- **Verifier cost on-chain**: STARK verification is hash-heavy; SNARK
  verification is pairing-heavy. Which is cheaper *on Starknet* depends on
  what the chain can verify natively — see Part 4.

## Why Starknet's stack is STARK-based

Three compounding reasons, each verified in this repo's history:

1. **Cairo is STARK-native.** Cairo's execution model was designed to
   produce STARK traces; the toolchain (Part 4) speaks this language
   natively.
2. **No ceremony.** A permissionless system whose security rests on a
   ceremony is a harder sell than one resting on hashes.
3. **Starknet verifies STARK proofs at the protocol level** (the PROOF1 /
   PROOF2 path), so a user-proven STARK attached to a transaction costs
   ~zero *contract* gas — whereas verifying a SNARK in-contract costs
   ~150–270M gas (measured in this repo's Garaga experiments).

The historical attempt in the other direction is instructive: expressing
felt252/Poseidon arithmetic *inside* a SNARK needed 47k constraints per
Poseidon permutation (measured), and a full statement's compiler OOM'd at
~99 GB. Emulation is the tax that made the STARK path win.

## What to carry forward

1. SNARKs: hiding native, proofs tiny, setup trusted, foreign fields slow.
2. STARKs: transparent, hash-fast, proofs chunky, hiding must be engineered.
3. Starknet's infrastructure pays for STARK verification natively — which is
   why this project's proving stack is S-two, and why hiding is the open
   problem rather than the default.
